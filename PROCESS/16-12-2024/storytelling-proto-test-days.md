# Story

Once upon a time, in the heart of a vast forest, there stood a Giant Tree. Its majestic branches reached so high that they almost touched the clouds, and its roots, as thick as rivers, plunged so deep that they shook the earth. The tree was the soul of the forest. Its leaves whispered ancient secrets in the wind, its fruit fed all the inhabitants, and its shade offered shelter to those in need.

Every year, at the time of renewal, the creatures of the forest - colourful birds, cunning foxes, swift squirrels and silent snakes - gathered around him. They would listen to the wind singing in its leaves, a gentle song that told the story of the world. In return, they would leave their most beautiful offerings: freshly fallen petals and joyful songs. In return, the tree offered them its fruit and its eternal wisdom.

But one day, intruders arrived from beyond the mountains. They wore armour as bright as iron and tools as sharp as lightning. They looked at the Tree, not with respect, but with greed. For them, the Tree was simply a resource, something to be taken.
The intruders moved into the forest and began to take what they wanted. They said: ‘This tree is ours. We've travelled a thousand miles to find it’. The inhabitants protested: ‘You can't own the Tree. It is the link between us all. But the intruders laughed at them. They planted iron posts and installed machines to strip the bark.

Day after day, they exploited the tree: they cut its branches for wood, tore out its roots to extract hidden treasures. They gathered all its fruits, but never shared them. Little by little, the tree lost its leaves, which fell like tears to the dry ground. The wind, once melodious, began to howl between the bare branches. The animals fell silent, replaced by the noise of machines. Some inhabitants left the forest in despair, while others stood by helplessly, watching the Tree wither away.

One silent morning, the intruders left. They had taken everything they could. All that remained was a ravaged landscape: blackened stumps, trenches dug by their machines, and in the centre, the Tree, reduced to a grey, withered trunk. The inhabitants, their hearts heavy, returned to it. They placed their paws, wings and hands on its dead bark. Silence seemed to engulf the whole forest.

But in the hollow of the trunk, they saw a faint glimmer. A seed, tiny but alive, lay there like a last breath of hope. They picked it up carefully and planted it in the parched earth. Every day, they watered it with their tears and their forgotten songs.

Days passed, and one morning, a small green shoot sprang from the earth. The inhabitants gathered around it and made a promise to each other: ‘Never again will we let the Tree be harmed’. The shoot grew slowly, lulled by the gentle wind. Little by little, the forest came back to life: the rivers murmured again, the birds came back to sing, and the roots spread into the earth.

The spirit of the Tree lived on, not as a memory, but as a renewal. The forest was reborn, fragile but protected, the guardian of a promise of harmony regained.
